Reassessment / common rules · previous placements removed

Topics from the corpus.

The main comparison uses DOI-indexed journal and proceedings articles first published between 1 January 2020 and 17 October 2026. The same author-search budget, ORCID supplementation, institutional-source checks, date/type rules and duplicate screening apply to every researcher. Books, chapters, theses, patents, standalone essays, preprints, editorials, cover items and forthcoming records are excluded.

An independent review receives only hashed paper IDs and titles/subtitles: no author names, affiliations, previous scores, map positions or preferred placements. It develops one corpus-grounded topic codebook and applies it to every title. Primary and related topics are the three highest mean ratings, with no researcher-specific overrides. Jonathan’s red hatch is a display convention only.

Score = mean(central = 1, supporting = 0.5, absent = 0) ×100 across coded titles. Radius is linear: 0 at the center, 100 at the outer scoring ring. Primary focus is the highest score, with no placement exceptions. Unresolved titles stay outside the denominator. This is one independent semantic review, not an inter-rater-validated or full-text assessment. It reduces earlier placement bias while retaining sampling, title-language and reviewer limitations.

Field notes / 002 — scales of investigation

Scales of investigation.

A fresh, author-blinded scale review of the same publication corpus. Compare everyone on one logarithmic axis, or filter by the newly coded research topics. Whiskers show mapped scope, the dot its geometric mean, and the hatch ±1 sample standard deviation in log space.

Scale is coded independently from topic: thermal research does not automatically mean building scale, and material research does not automatically mean nanoscale. Titles are assigned conventional physical-scope bands only when an object of investigation is identifiable; insufficient evidence stays unresolved. These bands are interpretive bins, not measured dimensions. Each mapped paper contributes one equally weighted mean of its band midpoints in log₁₀(metres). The dot and ±1 sample SD summarize those paper values, not measurement uncertainty or a confidence interval. Gaps between mapped bands do not imply work at intermediate scales.
